# Training Essential — image-65 — Editorial Satire (AI jet on a broken cart)

Mode: D — Editorial Satire
Score: 9.5/10 for colouring for its purpose (serious satire, not brightness)
Artist: Andreas Horn (credit the artist when reusing or publishing this reference)
Original file in this repo: `image-65.png`

## What it is

Editorial satire cartoon, landscape, ink and watercolour — not flat cartoon.
Speech bubble at top: "GOOD NEWS. WE CAN NOW DO THE WRONG THING A THOUSAND TIMES FASTER."
Illustration: a huge, shiny, modern jet engine labelled "AI" is strapped with rusty
straps onto a broken wooden cart labelled "BROKEN PROCESS". The cart has square
wooden wheels, splintered wood, loose bolts on the dirt ground. The jet fires with
blue flame and white smoke to the right, but the cart cannot move properly.

Three people on the left, each with a different reaction:
- The boss in the middle, blue suit, red tie, laughing, arm open, presenting the
  engine like a great success.
- The colleague on the far left, grey suit, worried, hand on chin, arms crossed.
- The worker at the front, dirty work clothes and a cap, crouching, hand on his
  head, trying to fix the broken wheel. Red toolbox and a wrench on the dirt.

The joke: if your process is broken, AI does not fix it — it just makes the same
wrong thing faster. The picture proves the words.

## Palette (approximate)

Muted and textured, not bright and flat: silver grey engine, brown wood cart,
beige dirt ground, pale blue sky, grey suits. Almost no bright colour at all.
Blue suit #2F5D8A (boss — the only person in strong blue), red tie and red
toolbox #C0392B (tiny accents only, linking boss and worker), blue jet flame
#3A7BD5 (the only other blue, repeats the boss's suit), black ink outlines,
white smoke, white speech bubble. Everything else grey, brown, beige.

## What it teaches

- Use a visual metaphor, not a literal illustration. Do not draw "AI is fast".
  Draw a jet engine on a broken cart. One strong metaphor beats ten details.
- Contrast materials to tell the story: shiny new silver engine vs rough old
  brown wood, clean blue suit vs dirty work clothes, fast flame vs square wheels
  that cannot roll.
- Limit colour even more when the idea is serious. A muted palette plus two tiny
  accents is stronger than six bright colours. Muted is the point here — this
  mode is the explicit exception to the "no muted grey-brown colours" line in
  the Section 10 negative prompt.
- Show three reactions in one frame: excited, worried, exhausted. No extra text
  is needed to explain who wins and who loses.
- Keep text to 3 pieces maximum: the joke (speech bubble), and two labels that
  are part of the scene ("AI" on the engine, "BROKEN PROCESS" on the cart).
  All in black, bold, easy to read. No extra caption, no logo, no small print.
- Make the hero object detailed and the background simple: dense detail on the
  engine and cart (wood grain, rivets, bolts, hatching, dirt, stones, smoke),
  simple background (pale sky, a few clouds, dirt ground). Black bars top and
  bottom frame it like a film still and focus the eye in the middle.
- Let the flaw be visible: square wheels, loose bolts, broken wood, a worker
  fixing it. The flaw is the joke — do not hide it or make it pretty.
- Composition / eye path: speech bubble first, then the AI engine (biggest,
  shiniest object), then "BROKEN PROCESS" on the cart below it, then the worker
  at the bottom, the real victim of the joke. The bubble points down to the
  boss, so you know who is speaking. The engine points right, with flame and
  smoke, so you feel speed. The cart is low and heavy, so you feel it cannot move.

## Flaws / limits to not repeat

- This is a serious, satirical mood. Do not reuse this muted palette for a
  funny joke post — that is Mode B's job. Mode D is for satire and critique.
- Do not add more people, objects, or text. Three reactions and three text
  pieces are the whole story.
- Do not brighten the palette "to make it pop". Brightening it breaks the mood.
- Do not clean up the mess: dirt, stones, loose bolts, and broken wood make the
  scene feel real. A tidy version loses the joke.
